# Propwash FPV – landing page, roadmap and drone configurator

Static website for **Propwash FPV**, an FPV drone racing and freestyle mod for Minecraft 26.3
(Fabric and NeoForge), with an online drone configurator for Propwash and its official add-ons **Propwash: Just More
Parts**, **Propwash: Camera Drones** and **Propwash: Mob Defense**.
This repository contains only the website, no mod source code.

## Structure

- `index.html` – the landing page (English and German, switchable), see [Landing page](#landing-page)
- `configurator/index.html` – the drone configurator (English and German, same switch)
- `convert/index.html` – the converter of old share codes (PW1, PW2 → PW3) for whole posts (English and German, same switch)
- `addons/index.html` and `addons/just-more-parts/index.html` – the add-on directory and the page of Just More Parts
  (English and German, same switch), see [Add-on directory](#add-on-directory)
- `sim/index.html` – the FPV simulator: flies the configurator's drone in the browser with the mod's flight model, see
  [FPV simulator](#fpv-simulator)
- `docs/` – the documentation (propwashfpv.com/docs, English and German): overview, player guide, world rules
  (gamerules), add-on API, API reference and parts format; generated by `tools/build-docs.mjs`, never edited by hand
- `roadmap/index.html` – the roadmap (English and German, same switch), drawn from `assets/data/roadmap.json`
- `changelog/index.html` – the changelog of every release of Propwash FPV and Just More Parts (English and German, same
  switch), drawn from `assets/data/changelog.json`, which `tools/build-changelog.mjs` generates from the mods' `CHANGELOG.md`
- `impressum/index.html` and `datenschutz/index.html` – the legal notice and privacy policy required under German law
  (English and German, same switch), linked from the footer of every page; the bracketed placeholders need filling in
  before the site goes live
- `brand/index.html` – the brand kit for creators (English and German, same switch, translated like the pages for
  players), linked from the footer of every page as “Brand kit” / “Presse & Brand”, see [Brand kit](#brand-kit)
- `assets/css/style.css` – styles of all pages; `assets/css/configurator.css` – configurator only;
  `assets/css/docs.css` and `assets/js/docs.js` – docs only (menu, tables, code blocks with copy buttons);
  `assets/css/roadmap.css` – roadmap only; `assets/css/changelog.css` – changelog only
- `assets/js/main.js` – language switch, main navigation (menu button and Features menu), hero feed with pause switch, image switchers, video demo, FAQ direct links, gallery lightbox
- `assets/js/configurator/` – the configurator as plain ES modules:
  - core: `data.js` (catalog), `sharecode.js` (the entry point that reads PW1, PW2 and PW3 and writes PW3, plus the frozen
    PW1 codec), `sharecode2.js` + `sharecode2-tables.js` (the frozen PW2 codec and the layers PW3 shares with it),
    `sharecode3.js` (PW3: codec, parts tables, add-on directory, conversion), `convert.js` (codes in running text),
    `crc32.js`, `inflate.js`, `tune.js`,
    `rules.js` (compatibility), `analysis.js` (flight analysis as in Propwash 0.6.0: energy balance, battery model, climb thrust and the heat model of motors and ESC), `fpv.js` (FPV camera rig and props in view),
    `tuning.js` (tune defaults and editing), `camdrones.js` (camera drones of Propwash: Camera Drones: what makes a
    build one, the check of the workbench button “Check camera drone”), `stand-ins.js` (the site's own textures for
    textures of Minecraft that an add-on model uses and no export carries), `index.js`
  - page: `ui.js` (part pickers, builds for beginners, paint, tune editor, OSD layer, stats with explanations, share
    code, import), `i18n.js` (texts), `assembly.js` (where every part sits on the drone, what a plugged-in battery
    hides), `paint.js` (paint tints), `viewer.js` (three.js viewer), `convert-page.js` (the page `/convert/`)
- `assets/js/roadmap/` – the roadmap page: `data.js` (loading, status counts and the validation shared with the test),
  `view.js` (horizons, release groups with progress rings, item cards, download buttons), `platforms.js` (the download
  platforms, also used by `tools/build-platforms.mjs`)
- `assets/js/changelog/` – the changelog page: `data.js` (loading, the entries' inline Markdown and the validation shared
  with the test), `view.js` (filter, version list, release cards, copy link)
- `assets/data/roadmap.json` – what the roadmap shows (see [Roadmap](#roadmap))
- `assets/data/platforms.json` – where Propwash and Just More Parts are downloaded and whether a version is still in
  review (see [Platforms](#platforms)); `assets/js/roadmap/platforms.js` reads it for the build script, the roadmap
  and the tests
- `assets/data/changelog.json` – what the changelog shows, generated (see [Changelog](#changelog))
- `assets/data/configurator/` – data from the web export of Propwash (format 2, with Just More Parts, Camera Drones and
  Mob Defense): `catalog.json`
  (parts, presets, rules, paint, tune, the parts table of every namespace and the add-on directory), `render.json` (frame
  layouts, motor seats, paint channels) and `models/` (one file per part: block-model geometry plus textures and plug
  anchors, loaded only for the parts on the drone); written by hand: `stathelp.json` (typical range of every figure per
  drone class) and `beginner.json` (the builds for beginners) with their pictures in `beginner/`, and
  `camera-drones.json` (what Camera Drones keeps in its code: nav boards with their sensors, gimbals with tilt, pan and
  lenses, the flight classes with the speeds of Cine, Normal and Sport)
- `assets/js/sim/` – the FPV simulator: `model/` (Propwash's flight model, `de.lardtom.propwash.sim`, ported 1:1 to JS,
  one file per Java class), `world.js` (the map and its voxel grid), `flight.js` (steps the model and moves the drone
  through the blocks), `eye.js` (keeps the FPV camera out of blocks), `scene.js` (three.js view), `osd.js`, `input.js`
  (radio, gamepad, keyboard), `setup.js` (controller dialog), `i18n.js`, `app.js`; `assets/css/sim.css`
- `assets/data/sim/map.glb` – the simulator's map (a Minecraft schematic exported from Blender, 1 unit = 1 m, 70 × 90 m)
- `assets/vendor/three/` – three.js r186 as a trimmed, minified bundle (MIT, `LICENSE`)
- `assets/vendor/pako/` – the deflate part of pako 2.1.0 (MIT, `LICENSE`), so share codes are byte-identical to the mod's
- `assets/img/` – real in-game screenshots as WebP (800 and 1600 px wide, plus cropped shots) and `og-image.jpg` for link
  previews, converted by `tools/build-landing-images.mjs` (see [Landing page](#landing-page)); `assets/img/docs/` – the
  pictures of the docs pages
- `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` – every page for search engines (see [Link check](#link-check))
- `assets/fonts/` – Saira and JetBrains Mono (Latin with Latin Extended-A), Exo 2 and JetBrains Mono for the Cyrillic
  letters of the Russian pages, all under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (see [Languages](#languages)); Barlow Condensed
  Black Italic and ExtraBold Italic (Basic Latin, OFL) only for the font samples of the brand kit page
- `tools/site-nav.mjs` – the main navigation and the footer links of every page, defined once (see
  [Navigation](#navigation))
- `tools/` – build and test scripts for the navigation, the landing page pictures, the configurator data and picture, the
  docs, the changelog, the roadmap check, the link check and the cache-busting stamps (`tools/stamp-assets.mjs`, see
  [Cache busting](#cache-busting); not needed at runtime)

No build step, no external requests, no cookies, no tracking. The chosen language and the hero's pause switch are
kept in the browser's `localStorage`; the drone built in the configurator lives only in the page address
(`configurator/#PW3-…`; old links `#PW2…` and `#PW1-…` still open and switch to PW3).

## Landing page

`index.html` is written by hand, English and German side by side (`<span lang="en">` / `<span lang="de">`, pictures
with `alt` and `data-de-alt`). It is for players who do not know Propwash yet; details live in the docs. Sections, in
this order (the ids are what the “Features” menu and older links use, so they stay):

| Section | id | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | `#top` | what it is, Download and the configurator as the two buttons, version line, rotating FPV feed |
| Get started | `#how` | four steps from building to racing, then the Flight School (`#learn`) for new pilots |
| Online configurator | `#configurator` | what the configurator does, its picture, the button to open it |
| What’s new | `#new` | highlights of the latest feature release (0.6.0: three, then three of its last fix block) and its changelog link |
| Flight &amp; tuning | `#flight` | flight model, tuning, the in-game configurator, the four ways to fly |
| Video | `#video` | analog and digital video and how they break up (interactive) |
| Racing &amp; freestyle | `#racing` | race line, gates, ghost, spectating, tricks, course building |
| Survival | `#survival` | crafting, loot, batteries, wear and repair |
| More details | `#extras` | six tiles: lights, action cams, paint, sound and dust, bandos, a world that reacts |
| Screenshots | `#gallery` | pictures the page does not show elsewhere |
| FAQ | `#faq` | questions before the download, each with its own id (`#faq-goggles`, `#faq-license`, …) for links from the Discord |
| Download | `#download` (old: `#release`) | version, platforms, compatibility, community (`#community`), add-on (`#addon`), source code and license (`#source`) |

A new feature gets a line or a tile in the section it belongs to, not a section of its own; what only matters for one
release goes into **What’s new** and moves into its section (or out) with the next release. For a release:

- **What’s new** (`#new`) gets the three highlights of the release with one picture each (0.6.0 has a second row of
  three for its last fix block: trick tutorials, advancements, replays as video) and links to
  `changelog/#propwash-<version>`; the version line in the hero names the release and links to `#new`; numbers such as
  tricks, lessons and world rules come from the mod's `CHANGELOG.md`. Before the release, the cards may stand without
  pictures and link to `changelog/#propwash`: the pictures come from the screenshots of the release commit and the
  anchor of the version only exists once `assets/data/changelog.json` has it (`tools/test-links.mjs` checks both)
- the versions and the review status come from `assets/data/platforms.json` (see [Platforms](#platforms)); the
  download section names the add-on Just More Parts with its version and part count, and the compatibility lines of
  the changelog (`tools/test-platforms.mjs` checks that the version in “Install” and in the add-on block is current)
- every FAQ question is a `<details>` with an id starting with `faq-`; `tools/test-platforms.mjs` knows the list of
  ids, so a new question also goes there; a direct link (`/#faq-license`, also by a hashchange) opens its question
  (`assets/js/main.js`, checked by `tools/test-faq.mjs`)
- the pictures are the mod's own screenshots of the release commit:

```sh
node tools/build-landing-images.mjs --propwash <propwash repository>   # assets/img/*.webp and og-image.jpg
```

The list of pictures is `IMAGES` in that script: name, screenshot, widths and, for cut-outs, the crop in screenshot
pixels (windows are cut out at their real size, drone close-ups are cut around the drone because drones are shown at
their real size since 0.5.0). Pictures also used by the docs (`assets/img/docs/`) are not converted twice. After a
change of a window's layout in the mod, check the cut-outs by eye. Needs ImageMagick and `cwebp`.

The picture of the configurator section is the configurator itself (3D view and flight figures of the default preset,
English, standing still), taken in headless Chrome:

```sh
node tools/build-configurator-shot.mjs   # assets/img/configurator-preview-640.webp and -1200.webp
```

Run it again after the configurator or its default preset changes; `--code <share code>` shows another drone,
`--zoom <steps>` zooms in like the <kbd>+</kbd> key (default 2). Needs Google Chrome and `cwebp`.

## Navigation

The header of every page has the same navigation: a **Features** menu with the sections of the landing page (title and
one line each), **Docs**, **Changelog**, **Roadmap**, **Download** and the **Configurator** as the highlighted button;
Discord sits next to the language switch (in the small-screen menu as a link). The footer of every page has the same
link line. Both are defined once in `tools/site-nav.mjs`:

```sh
node tools/build-nav.mjs           # writes navigation and footer links into the hand-written pages
node tools/build-nav.mjs --check   # lists pages that are out of date, writes nothing
node tools/test-nav.mjs            # every page up to date, menu entries point to sections, old anchors still there
```

The docs pages take both from the same module when they are rendered (`node tools/build-docs.mjs --render-only`). A
new hand-written page goes into `HAND_PAGES` of `tools/build-nav.mjs`; a full-window app page without navigation and
footer (the simulator) goes into `APP_PAGES` instead, and `test-nav.mjs` checks it has none. Below 1100 px the navigation folds behind the
menu button; the width is in `assets/css/style.css` and `assets/js/main.js` and `test-nav.mjs` checks that both agree.
The Features menu opens on click, tap or keyboard (Escape closes it, focus returns to its button), without JavaScript
on hover and focus; without JavaScript narrow screens show the links as a row below the brand.

## Drone configurator

`configurator/` builds a drone from every part of Propwash and of its official add-ons Just More Parts, Camera Drones and
Mob Defense:

- add-ons in the part lists: every part of an add-on carries its badge (“JMP”, “Camera Drones”, “Mob Defense”, else
  “Add-on”, the tooltip names the mod), and when a list holds parts of more than one source, the filter “Add-on” above
  it shows only those of Propwash or of one add-on; the preset list has a group per add-on with presets
- camera drones (Propwash: Camera Drones): the three drones Compact, Tour and Cine as presets and all 19 parts – folding
  frames, nav flight controllers, folding props, motors, camera links, smart batteries and gimbals – with model, mass,
  the figures of the workbench and the help texts of the game; the part lists name what a nav board and a gimbal bring
  (GPS, obstacle sensors, tilt range, 360° pan, lenses) instead of FPV figures, a camera drone gets the panel “Check
  camera drone” (the workbench button of the add-on, row by row like its screen: Nav-FC, gimbal, battery and camera link
  fitted, other, missing or wrong; a wrong Nav-FC says why, a missing or wrong part names the part of the frame's
  camera drone to fit, an other part that is fine the recommended one; “No camera frame” only while the build is
  incomplete; what the nav board, gimbal and flight class bring)
  and its figures are compared with camera drones, not with an FPV class (`stathelp.json`, `build_classes`); the cine
  frame has its class Cinelifter; the FPV camera preview says that a camera drone films with its gimbal camera
- Mob Defense: the dispenser mount (front, four slots), spear mount (bottom) and rocket booster (top) as accessories
  with mass, mount and the frame limits of the game (only on a frame with that mount, e.g. not the dispenser on the
  65 mm whoop); the front plate of the dispenser uses Minecraft's own dispenser texture, which no export and no jar
  carries – the 3D view draws it with a texture of the site's own (`stand-ins.js`, named in the model file's
  `stand_ins`)

- builds for beginners above the parts: three Propwash presets (`assets/data/configurator/beginner.json`) with a picture,
  their key figures, “Load” and “Copy code” (the PW3 code is written when copied, so it stays right when a table grows),
  and the hint to start in Angle mode with a link to the Flight School
- part pickers per category with search, a class filter (whoop, toothpick, cinewhoop, freestyle, race, long range,
  X-Class; video by link, accessories by mount), “only parts that fit”, fit badges and tips with pros, cons and what the
  part would change
- live flight figures like the workbench of Propwash 0.6.0 (weight, thrust-to-weight, hover throttle, top speed and its
  energy balance: flight times down to the landing voltage for mixed flying of the frame's class as the main value,
  aggressive/racing, hovering and cruising at the class's cruise speed, pack currents, landing voltage under load,
  base load of flight controller and video, motor temperature after 60 s full throttle and after a mixed flight at
  20 °C, heat warning when the motors or the ESC overheat at full throttle in place) and
  the compatibility check (voltage, prop size vs. frame, battery fit, mounts, ESC and battery load); every figure has an
  info button that opens an explanation on hover, focus or a tap (Escape closes it) with the typical range of the
  frame's class (`assets/data/configurator/stathelp.json`), and a value outside that range gets a quiet ring – not an
  error, errors are the compatibility check's. The explanations are in `i18n.js` (`statHelp`), so the other languages
  pick them up with the configurator's texts
- paint for every paint slot the build shows, like the paint screen in the game: the 16 dye colours, the 5 finishes
  (carbon, gunmetal, aluminium, gold, copper) and a custom colour; a swatch paints exactly its RGB value, as in the mod;
  “Randomize” paints all slots with a random but matching scheme (accent and partner colour, dark/light/metal base,
  props alike or front/rear for orientation)
- tune editor for rates (with a live rate-curve graph), PID, feedforward, TPA, filters and throttle curve (with graph),
  using the defaults the game derives from the build
- optional OSD layer (a Propwash OSD preset or the layout from an opened code)
- share codes like Propwash 0.6.0 (`pw3.md` of the mod, PW3 revision 3.1): the page writes PW3 and reads PW3, PW2, PW1
  and the old PWD1 codes of the drone editor; codes are byte-identical to the game's. Opening an old link (`#PW2…`, `#PW1-…`) or pasting an old code loads the build, switches
  the address to its PW3 code (the one `/propwash code convert` gives in the game, unless a part of the old code has been
  replaced since) and offers “Copy PW3”.
  A PW3 code names every add-on it uses; one this page does not have shows as “Needs *name* *version*” with its Modrinth
  or CurseForge link (from the add-on directory, else from the code; a free address in a code is only shown as text),
  a code for another version of a loaded add-on as “doesn't match the loaded version”. Unknown parts keep their place
  and are written back unchanged. Tuning-preset codes (`PWT1`) get a hint where they belong. A creative drone keeps its
  values (layer “Creative values”, PW3 extension 0): the banner says “Creative drone: N own values, M base parts”, the
  share box writes them back unchanged (or without them when the layer is unticked), and the check list names the
  Creative Flight Controller instead of an unknown part – the page can't rate a creative drone. A PWD1 code (or a link
  `#PWD1-…`) opens like an old code: the Flight School's freestyle drone with the Creative Flight Controller and the
  code's values, the address switches to its PW3 code and “Copy PW3” is offered
- converter `/convert/`: paste any text – a whole Discord post – and every PW1, PW2 and PWD1 code in it (also inside
  links) becomes PW3 in place (a PWD1 code with its creative values), at most 100 codes per pass; below, one row per code with name, class, status (converted, already
  PW3, unknown parts, can't be converted with the reason, damaged), the new code to copy and a link into the
  configurator. Nothing is uploaded; the page uses the same modules as the configurator, without the 3D view
- 3D view with three.js: the parts' Minecraft block models, textures and paint tints placed like the mod's drone
  renderer (frame layouts, motor seats, camera tilt, accessory anchors); drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom,
  auto-rotate (off with reduced motion or paused animations), keyboard control on the canvas; with the battery in, the
  drone shows no plugs and no power leads like in Propwash 0.6.0 (`PowerLead`, `PlugMask`): the stack is drawn as its
  `_mounted` variant without its two pigtails and the battery without its socket and the leads to it (the elements the
  game leaves out by name, found by the socket box and the pigtail ends of the model data) – without a `_mounted`
  variant or a socket both parts are drawn as before; the VTX antennas sit on the frame's antenna holder like in
  Propwash 0.6.0 (`assets/js/configurator/antenna.js`, ports of `AntennaRules`, `AntennaGeometry` and `AntennaMask`):
  the holder is the frame's `antenna_anchor` from `parts.json` (position and tilt, 45° by default; without one the
  fallback from the frame's geometry right at the rear, 12 mm behind the top plate, on a whoop 0.18 × wheelbase; a
  point `antenna` in `drone_layout.json` still moves it), the antennas come from the VTX's `antenna` (count, length and
  cap, else derived from range, mass, video system, image quality and latency: whip, stubby, lollipop (race 45–60 mm),
  two lollipops in a V for HD, pagoda for long range), at most as long as the frame's `max_length_mm` (1.2 ×
  wheelbase), one in the middle straight up and back, a pair in a V from one point leaning out by the frame's
  `pair_lean_deg` each (15°, narrower on small open frames; both computed from the frame when an older export lacks
  them); a pack on top that reaches further back than the holder moves the antenna 5 mm behind its rear (the pack
  without its plug and leads) and the TPU holder reaches forward to the top plate by as much; drawn from shapes –
  holder, SMA, sleeve, stalk and cap – not tilted with the camera, picked as part of the video part, their caps
  painted with the slot “Antenna tips” (which starts with the cap's colour) and hidden in the camera preview; the VTX
  model leaves out the faces of its elements named `antenna…` (add-on VTX, listed as `antenna` on the model file's
  base variant), so no antenna is drawn twice – a VTX model file without that list (data of an older export) keeps its
  antennas and gets none at the holder; a VTX without external antennas (Propwash 0.6.0-rc.3, `VideoAntenna.none()`:
  `"antenna": "none"` in its part file, `count` 0 in the web export) gets neither antennas nor holder and no slot
  “Antenna tips” from them, its mass and figures stay as they are
- FPV camera preview (switch “3D / FPV camera” in the viewer, or open `configurator/?view=camera#PW1-…`): the image the
  drone's camera sends, drawn like the game's FPV view (since Propwash 0.4.0): 16:9 rectilinear from the lens with the drone's
  camera angle and the field of view of the camera in the video unit (`camera_fov_deg`, e.g. analog race 125°, digital
  lite 104°, HD 130°), the drone itself hidden except its props, spinning props as translucent blur discs in their paint
  colour, over a sky and grass backdrop; a camera-angle slider limited to the frame's camera mount (e.g. race 15–70°,
  freestyle 0–50°, as in the drone's service menu in the game) with the live props-in-view share – the angle is stored in
  the drone's tune and travels in the share code (PW1 revision 1.2) – the camera's field of view read-only, and toggles to
  mark the counted disc area, show still blades or draw the frame too
- screenshot menu in the 3D view: “Drone as PNG” (the drone alone, transparent, cropped, up to 1600 px, e.g. as the cover
  image of a forum post) and “Detail card” (1600 × 900 with name, class, key figures and share code); both are copied to
  the clipboard, or downloaded where the browser can’t copy images. Both always show the drone from the same angle,
  whatever the 3D view shows: from the front right and above (`SHOT_AZIMUTH` 34.75° from the nose towards the right
  side, `SHOT_ELEVATION` 27.75° in `viewer.js`), nose to the lower right, all four props in sight
- picking in the 3D view and the FPV image: a click or tap on a part opens its category with the current part card
  focused (Parts tab) or makes its paint slot the target (Paint tab); the mouse pointer highlights and names the part
  (in the Paint tab the faces of the slot, and on a face without a tint group of its own also the part as a whole, a
  little), drags, long presses and pinches never select; the plug and lead belong to the stack and are not paintable
  (`tools/test-configurator-pick.mjs` clicks every part in headless Chrome)

### Updating the data

The data come from the web export of Propwash (format 2, `/propwash webexport all` with the add-ons installed). The
current data: the web exports of the release build of Propwash 0.6.0 (`release/0.6.0/web-export/` of the release:
`propwash-fpv`, `just-more-parts` with Just More Parts 1.2.0 and `camera-drones` with Camera Drones 1.0.0, each made
by `runWebExport` of Propwash 0.6.0 with that add-on) and Mob Defense 1.0.0 read from its release jar with
`tools/build-addon-export.mjs` (its release brings only its own export in format 1, the raw part files); `catalog.json`
names them in `source.sources` (generator, time, namespaces) and the jars with their SHA-256 in `source.jars`:

```sh
# in a Propwash checkout of the release stand, with the add-on jars in one folder
./gradlew :fabric:runWebExport -PpropwashWebExportMods=<folder with the add-on jars>
# an add-on without a format-2 export: read it from its jar like the configurator's jar loader does
node tools/build-addon-export.mjs --jar <add-on jar> --out <folder>   # then pass <folder> as one more --export
```

The build scripts read one export with all add-ons or several exports (`--export` repeated, or `JMP_WEB_EXPORT` with
paths separated like `PATH`): `base/` comes from the first export that has it, every namespace from the first export
that has it. Default: the newest `web-export` of `../propwash-justmoreparts/release/<version>/`,
`../propwash-camera/release/<version>/` and `../propwash-mobdefense/release/<version>/` that exist. `--mods <jar or
folder>` (or `PROPWASH_WEB_EXPORT_MODS`) gives the jars of the add-ons: their metadata (`fabric.mod.json` or
`neoforge.mods.toml`) gives each namespace its homepage, which a PW3 code names for an add-on that is not in the add-on
directory (Camera Drones and Mob Defense 1.0.0: their CurseForge pages, link type 2 of PW3, clickable; their rc.1 jars
named `propwashfpv.com`, only as text) – without it the page's codes of those add-ons would differ from the game's.
The PW3 vectors of every export with `base/` are kept (each name once; the same name must have the same code), so
`tools/fixtures/pw3-export-vectors.json` holds the game's codes of all 31 presets (62 vectors), and
`build-configurator-heat.mjs` reads several exports the same way:

```sh
node tools/build-configurator-data.mjs --export <dir> --mods <jars> --goldens <propwash>/common/src/test/resources/sharecode
                                           # catalog.json, the export's PW3 vectors and the golden files of Propwash
node tools/build-configurator-models.mjs --export <dir> --resources <propwash>/common/src/main/resources --resources <unpacked JMP jar>
                                           # render.json and models/ (with the plug anchors and the props'
                                           # blur models for the simulator)
node tools/build-configurator-heat.mjs --export <dir> … # heat figures of the game (tools/fixtures/heat-vectors.json)
node tools/build-addons.mjs --jar justmoreparts=<jar>   # the hosted export of Just More Parts the configurator shows
```

writes the game's heat figures of every preset and one-part-swapped variant into `tools/fixtures/heat-vectors.json`, so

Accessories that fit only some frames (Propwash 0.6.0: `frames` in the part file, `data.frames` in the web export, the
build problem `accessory_frame` in `rules.json`) are read from add-on jars, checked like the workbench and offered in the
accessory picker only on their frames. `catalog.json` already carries the rule `accessory_frame` with the game's texts

language files of that checkout, the dictionaries, the gimbals of an add-on checkout next to this one (if there) and the
pickers in headless Chrome.

Recessed accessories (Propwash 0.6.0: `recess_mm` and `drop_mm` in the part file and in `data` of the web export, only
when not 0) sit that far behind their anchor and that far lower, like an accessory that reaches back under the frame
instead of hanging in front of the nose. `assembly.js` draws the body at the game's `AccessoryAnchor#modelOrigin`
(`accessoryOrigin`: the anchor plus `[0, −drop_mm, recess_mm]` turned by the accessory's tilt; the base of the top
mount stays at the anchor), `analysis.js` moves the box for the centre of mass and the inertia like `AccessoryType`, and
the jar path reads and exports both fields like `PartJson` and `PartData`. An export without the fields draws and
analyses every accessory as before. `tools/test-configurator-render.mjs` checks every frame and mount, the analysis and
the jar path.

Straight-hanging accessories (Propwash 0.6.0: `hangs_straight: true` in `data` of the web export for the gripper and
every accessory with `hangs_straight` in its part file) hang without the mount's tilt and are not stretched to the
anchor length, like `AccessoryAnchor#tiltFor` and `#stretch`; an accessory with an LED path is not stretched either.
`assembly.js` (`accessoryTilt`, `accessoryStretch`, `accessoryOrigin`) and `analysis.js` (`tiltFor`, `anchorStretch`)
read the field, the jar path writes it like `PartData` (only when true, last). The web export of Propwash 0.6.0 brings
it for the gripper (Propwash 0.6.0-rc.4, FBW-1). With it, `freestyle_5` with the gripper below gives
the game's figures (inertia Ixx 0.0015485, pitch authority 1819.1), checked by `tools/test-configurator-render.mjs`.

`tools/fixtures/report-vectors.json` keeps share codes from bug reports with the build, analysis and FPV camera the
mod gives for them (hand-maintained, not overwritten by the build scripts); the test decodes, re-encodes and analyses
each one. The first is the 7″ deadcat whose props the game showed at the image edges while the old calculation said
0.0 %; its `game` entry holds what Propwash's self test counted in the rendered FPV image (2.01 % of 1708 × 960 pixels),
and the test fails if the configurator ever shows 0 % for it again. The same code carries the second report (flight
times and part stats a bit off, e.g. 24.2 min hovering and 124 °C at full throttle): `analysis` holds the figures of
Propwash 0.5.2 and Just More Parts 1.1.1 (19.6 min hovering, 11.9 min cruising, 8.1 min mixed, 5.8 min aggressive)
with the motor temperatures of Propwash 0.6.0-rc.1 and Just More Parts 1.2.0-rc.1 (96 °C after 60 s full throttle), `analysis_before` the ones of an earlier pre-release build; the test checks that weight, thrust-to-weight, hover
throttle and props in view stay unchanged (the flight physics is bit-identical; the top speed moves with the climb thrust
of 0.5.0 and the battery model of 0.5.2) and that the energy figures changed.

Render definitions may carry a vanilla `transformation` (Just More Parts shrinks the X-Class frame models to fit
Minecraft's model limit and scales them back to real size this way); `build-configurator-models.mjs` bakes it into the
element coordinates, and the render test checks that every frame model reaches its motor positions.

## FPV simulator

`sim/` flies a drone in the browser the way it flies in the game. The configurator's **Fly** button (in the 3D view's
toolbar) opens `sim/#<share code>` with the drone as it is: parts, paint and tune, whatever the share code's layer
switches say; without a code the simulator flies the default preset (`justmoreparts:deadcat5_6s`).

- **Flight model:** `assets/js/sim/model/` is the mod's `de.lardtom.propwash.sim` package ported 1:1 (same class, field
  and method names; conventions at the top of each file): rigid body at 2 kHz, motors, ESCs and battery with sag and
  heat, rotor aerodynamics (inflow, translational lift, H-force, ground effect, prop wash, vortex ring, wake), body
  drag, wind and gusts, the Betaflight-style flight controller (Betaflight/Actual rates, PID with feed-forward, I-term
  relax, anti-gravity, TPA, airmode, Acro/Angle/Horizon, filters, gyro model), arming checks, ground and hull contact,
  crashes and prop strikes (crash disarm is off in the simulator). `AirframeSpec.fromBuild(build, catalog)` derives the airframe from the
  catalog parts (ParamDerivation, reusing `analysis.js`), `FlightControllerConfig.fromTune(tune, airframe)` the flight
  controller from the effective tune (`tuning.js`). Left out: GPS rescue, altitude and position hold (with the compass
  error and the takeover after a rescue), 3D mode, turtle mode, water, cobwebs, breaking blocks, part wear, add-on
  flight assists, axes and collision flags, entities as obstacles.
- **Flight model 0.6.0:** `assets/js/sim/model/` follows Propwash 0.6.0: crashes that do not disarm (props touching
  ground or walls lose thrust, the flight controller stays calm when the drone lies or was knocked over at idle), the
  support grid under the drone (tipping over edges, accessory stands), surfaces, ESC heat with its power limit and hot
  motors losing torque. `model/StrictMath.js` ports fdlibm so the model computes exp, atan2, acos, asin and pow like
  the JVM. The first port followed Propwash `main` (the 0.5.2 line); the classes of 0.6.0 that came after
  (`GroundSupport`, `SurfaceResponse`) are ported too.
- **Drone editor:** `AirframeSpec.of(build, catalog, creative)` derives an edited drone like
  `ParamDerivation#compute(source, flight, inertia)`: `creative = { build, catalog, inertia, hash? }` with the flight
  build, a catalog holding the edited parts and the inertia override `[roll, pitch, yaw]` in kg·m² (NaN for automatic).
- **Checks:** `node tools/test-sim-model.mjs` compares the model with the classes of the release jar: the mod's
  AirframeGoldenTest, every AirframeSpec field and 13 flights frame by frame, bit for bit (vectors in
  `tools/fixtures/sim/`, made by `node tools/build-sim-vectors.mjs --jar <release jar> --propwash <checkout>` with JDK
  25, without Minecraft). The configurator's `analysis.js` keeps JavaScript's `Math.pow`, which is 1 ulp off at three
  inputs (`kTorque` of 76 and 152 mm props, the cooling scale of two motors); the test computes those with the jar's
  pow and reports the difference.
- **World:** `world.js` turns the map into 0.5 m cells (every face marks the cell behind it); the ground continues at
  the map's floor level all around. `flight.js` runs the mod's pilot loop (environment probes, wind of a clear overworld
  day, frame slicing at speed, block clipping like `rescue.VoxelFlight`, `afterMove`), `eye.js` the FPV eye guard
  (`EyeClearance`). The start pad is `SPAWN` in `app.js`.
- **Views:** FPV from the lens with the drone's camera angle and the video unit's field of view and line of sight (`C`)
  with the drone's block models (`drone-model.js`, `createPartMeshes` of `viewer.js`); Betaflight-style OSD with the
  craft name.
- **Props** like Propwash 0.6.0 (`prop-spin.js`, `fpv-props.js`, `prop-view.js`): every prop turns with the rotor speed
  of its own motor in the flight model (QuadcopterEntity: 20 ticks per second, 0.9 rad per tick at idle up to 3.2 at
  full output, 35 % response per tick, drawn between the ticks) in the spin direction of that motor (props in: front
  left and rear right clockwise seen from above, the `_ccw` model on the other two). An armed prop whose motor output
  lies above the blur threshold 0.12 shows its blur model instead of the blades: a translucent disc in the slot's prop
  paint with a sharp hub and nut (the variants `_blur` and `_blur_paint` of the web export; `tl` in the model file names
  the translucent texture). In FPV the own drone is hidden; with **Props** on in the bar (the game's setting *Props in
  FPV view*, on by default, kept in `localStorage` as `propwash-sim-fpv-props`) the camera's own pass draws what the
  real camera would see (FpvProps): the props of the slots whose motor hangs on an arm of the frame and whose disc
  reaches into the image (blades, or the blur disc and the blur model's hub), the frame's arms and those motors, all
  placed from the lens, scaled by `nearScale`, clipped and pushed out like in the game, so they keep their place in the
  image. The duct rings of a whoop or cinewhoop show like the game's default *Ducts in FPV view* (on). Not like the
  game: every prop shows its own motor (Propwash 0.6.0 swaps front left and rear right when it draws them), no worn,
  damaged or broken props (no part wear), no video feed filter on the FPV image, and a camera drone's gimbal does not
  hide the props. `tools/test-sim-props.mjs` checks the formulas against the mod and the page in headless Chrome
  (`--shots <dir>` keeps its screenshots).
- **Input:** radios over USB (“USB Joystick (HID)”), gamepads (browser Gamepad API) and the keyboard, with the mod's
  defaults (AETR, AUX1 arm, AUX2 Acro/Horizon/Angle; gamepad Mode 2 with right shoulder arm; keyboard W/S A/D, arrows,
  `Z`, `R`, `Backspace`, `U`). The **Controller** dialog sets device, stick mode 1–4, channels (with detection),
  inversion, calibration, throttle mode and switches; profiles stay in the browser (`localStorage`,
  `propwash-sim-input`). Sound switch and volume slider in the bar (`propwash-sim-sound`, `propwash-sim-volume`). After a reset (`Backspace`) or flip the drone arms again as soon as the throttle is low while
  the arm switch stays on. `window.propwashSim` exposes the running sim for debugging in the console.
- **Note about the physics:** the page opens with a note (`[data-about]` in `sim/index.html`, bottom centre) that the
  physics are not 100 % the mod's, above all crashes (a crash never disarms here), and what is not included (the
  left-out list above in player words). It closes with *Got it*, Escape or the first arming and opens again with
  **Physics** in the bar; the help says the same. Note and help link the legal notice and the privacy policy, because
  the page has no footer. Keep the list in the note in step with the flight model.
- **Languages:** the page is an app page (`APP_PAGES` of `tools/build-nav.mjs`: head block, no navigation) and is
  translated like the other pages for players (`tools/i18n/pages.mjs`, `app: true`): the banner of the AI translation
  goes into the note at the marker `<!-- i18n-banner -->`, the scripts' texts are `['English', 'Deutsch']` pairs in
  `assets/js/sim/i18n.js` (source `js-arrays`), and on a translated page EN/DE open the English/German page with the
  same drone. The configurator's **Fly** link stays in the language tree.

```sh
node tools/test-sim-page.mjs   # note about the physics, help, legal links, bar at 390 px (headless Chrome, en/de and
                               # every generated language)
node tools/test-sim-fly.mjs    # airframes of every preset and game vector, the Fly link from the configurator, arming
```

## Documentation (`docs/`)

The docs pages describe one Propwash release and are rebuilt for every release from the Propwash source code:

```sh
# in the Propwash repository, on the release commit
tools/export_rules.sh                                        # build/web-export/gamerules.json
# here
node tools/build-docs.mjs --propwash <propwash repository> --images
node tools/test-docs.mjs
```

`--propwash` defaults to `PROPWASH_SRC` or the folder `propwash` next to this repository; `--gamerules <file>` points to
another export. `--images` converts the in-game screenshots the pages use (`IMAGES` in `tools/build-docs.mjs`, taken from
`docs/screenshots/` of the mod) to WebP in `assets/img/docs/`, 800 and 1600 px wide but never wider than the screenshot
(needs `cwebp`); `--image <key>` (repeatable) converts only these entries and keeps the others, and `--screenshots <dir>`
takes the screenshots from another folder, for example the unpacked screenshots of a release self-test run. Look at every
picture before it goes on the site. `--version <x.y.z>` overrides the version from `gradle.properties` for a preview of
a release that is not versioned yet (the preview docs of 0.6.0 came from a `0.6.0-dev` checkout with `--version 0.6.0`);
the real release build runs without it (0.6.0: from the release commit `bba73caea`, with the world rules exported from
an archive of that commit). `--render-only` rebuilds the pages from `tools/docs/data/` alone, without a
Propwash checkout.

What comes from the code (`tools/docs/extract.mjs`, written to `tools/docs/data/*.json`):

- **world rules**: the mod's export of its rule catalogue (`tools/export_rules.sh`): ids, types, defaults, ranges,
  groups, value names, example commands and the same English and German texts as in the game; also published as
  `docs/gamerules/gamerules.json`
- **API reference**: every public type and member of `de.lardtom.propwash.api`, read from the Java sources
  (`tools/docs/java-api.mjs`), the API version and the types from outside the package that the API exposes
- **parts format**: the field names the part reader (`parts/PartJson.java`) reads per category, the build problems and
  warnings in the order `build/DroneBuild.java` checks them, the enum values, the built-in mounts, parts and chemistries,
  and real part files as examples
- **keys and switch functions**: `KeyDefaults`, `SwitchFunction` and the language files
- **server.json**: settings, defaults and limits from `ServerRulesConfig`
- **race classification**: whether each world rule is race-relevant or neutral (`WorldRuleSpecs`), added to the world
  rules as `race_relevant`
- **rules for add-ons**: `ADDONS.md` of the mod (English, authoritative), shown as the section `#rules` of the add-on API
  page; the German version is `ADDON_RULES_DE` in `tools/docs/pages/api.mjs`, written in the same block structure and
  tied to the SHA-256 of the `ADDONS.md` it translates
- **versions**: mod, Minecraft, API, network protocol and parts format

The explanations are written in `tools/docs/pages/*.mjs` (English and German side by side). `tools/test-docs.mjs`
checks that the pages match the renderer, that every API type, part field, server setting and event has its
explanation (and none is left for something that is gone), that the API history covers the current API version, that
the API page lists the build problems and warnings of `DroneBuild` and names every accessory field added in Propwash
0.6.0 in its API 1.7 overview and in the history of API 1.7 or 1.8, that every example command is valid, that every world rule is classified for races, that the German rules for add-ons
follow the current `ADDONS.md` (hash and structure), and on every page: unique ids, working anchors and local links, English and German in
pairs, balanced tags, real umlauts in German, and no local paths or terms from the local denylist. When a release adds
something, the test names the file and the entry to write. The denylist is not part of this repository: the test reads
the same local file as the mod's public export (`PROPWASH_EXPORT_DENYLIST`, otherwise
`~/.config/propwash/public-export-denylist.txt`, one term per line) and fails without it.

`node tools/test-news-060.mjs` checks what the site says about the last fix block of Propwash 0.6.0, only what the
merged code does: the sections of the player guide (laps the server doesn't count with the pass zone warning, the race
line per course, replays as video, sculk, bosses fighting drones, zone outlines in flight, the join overview without
add-ons players don't need, mixed servers with Fabric as server loader, trick tutorials, *Unlock all* in the flight
training, the advancement tab) with their pictures, the world rules `drones_trigger_sculk` and
`drones_boss_interactions` (new in the release), the cards of “What’s new” and the FAQ about NeoForge and
Fabric players, the roadmap card of the camera drones, the changelog line about NeoForge players on Fabric servers, and
that every dictionary in `tools/i18n/dict` has every text of the site (nothing missing, nothing stale).

`node tools/test-protocol-texts.mjs` checks the network protocol of the described release: `PROTOCOL` in the test names
it per Propwash version (0.6.0: 20 in the release; rc.4 had 19, rc.3 had 18, rc.1 and rc.2 had 17), and the docs data,
`Constants.PROTOCOL_VERSION` at the Propwash commit the changelog page reads, every page under `docs/` (rows of other API
versions in the API history excepted), the changelog entries of that release in English and German and the dictionaries
must all name it. A release that raises the protocol adds its number there.

The docs menu groups the pages under **Play** and **Make add-ons** (`GROUPS` and `PAGES` in `tools/docs/html.mjs`); the
overview page shows the same groups as cards. A new docs page needs four things: an entry in `PAGES` with its path, id,
group and title, a builder in `tools/docs/pages/<id>.mjs`, its line in `BUILDERS` of `tools/build-docs.mjs`, and a card in
`tools/docs/pages/index.mjs` (the build stops and names the page while the card is missing). A new group is one more
entry in `GROUPS`.

The header navigation and the footer links of the docs pages come from `tools/site-nav.mjs` (see
[Navigation](#navigation)); after changing them, run `node tools/build-docs.mjs --render-only` (`test-docs.mjs` fails
until then). The license note of the API page is `LICENSE_NOTE` in `tools/docs/pages/api.mjs`.

## Platforms

Propwash and its add-ons are downloaded only from **CurseForge and Modrinth**. GitHub is never a download: it only
appears as the small source-code icon (named in `aria-label` and `title`, never in visible text), and nothing links to
`…/releases`. `assets/data/platforms.json` is the one place for the download addresses and their state:

```jsonc
{
  "schema": 1,
  "products": {                         // the first product is the mod itself, the others are add-ons
    "propwash": { "name": "Propwash FPV", "short": "Propwash FPV", "version": "0.6.0", "minecraft": "26.3",
      "curseforge": { "url": "https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/propwash-fpv", "status": "review" },
      "modrinth":   { "url": "https://modrinth.com/mod/propwash-fpv", "status": "review" } }
  },
  "source": { "propwash": "https://github.com/LardTom/propwash-fpv" },   // repository addresses, for the icon
  "labels": { … }                       // button and status texts in English and German, {placeholders}
}
```

- **Status** per platform and product, for the version named: `live` – the button “Download on …” (the first live
  platform of the mod is the highlighted one); `review` – a plain button with a dot “… · in review”, the platform still
  checks the files (keep it until the platform has released them); `pending` – no link, the line “Coming to …”, for a
  product that is not on the platform yet.
- **Landing page:** `tools/build-platforms.mjs` writes the generated parts between `<!-- platforms:<kind> <product> -->`
  and `<!-- /platforms -->`, so they are right without JavaScript: `hero` (the download button of the hero: the first
  live platform, otherwise “Download” to `#download`), `buttons` (platform buttons and the source-code icon), `status`
  (status line with the version), `faq-download` (the answer “Where can I download it?”) and `source` (the icon alone).
  Any hand-written page can carry such regions.
- **Roadmap:** a horizon with `"download": "<product>"` gets the buttons of that product, drawn at runtime from the same
  file.

```sh
node tools/build-platforms.mjs           # after editing platforms.json: rewrites the regions of every page
node tools/build-platforms.mjs --check   # lists out-of-date regions, writes nothing
node tools/test-platforms.mjs            # data, regions, links on every page, versions, FAQ ids
```

For a release: set the new `version`, `status: "review"` for every platform, run the build; once a platform has
released the files, set it to `live` and run the build again. `test-platforms.mjs` also checks every page, data file
and script: Propwash pages on CurseForge and Modrinth only with the addresses of `platforms.json` (the footer constants
in `tools/site-nav.mjs` and `SOURCE_JMP` of the docs included), repository links only below a `source` address, links
saying “Download” only to a platform, and on the player pages (not the docs and legal pages) no visible “GitHub”. It
also keeps the deferred server add-on off every page and out of the roadmap data, and checks the landing page: “What’s
new” links to `changelog/#propwash-<version>` and every card has a picture, the converter (`convert/`), the add-on
directory (`addons/`) and the rules for add-ons (`docs/api/#rules`) are linked where the FAQ and the license name them,
and the add-on block and the compatibility list say which version of Just More Parts the release needs.

## Add-on directory

`/addons/` lists the released add-ons of Propwash, official ones first (now: Just More Parts), community add-ons
below once lardtom has checked them against the rules for add-ons; `/addons/just-more-parts/` is the page of the
official add-on. Downloads come only from `assets/data/platforms.json` (CurseForge and Modrinth). Nothing is listed
before it is released: no page for an add-on still in development and none for the deferred server add-on.

- `assets/data/addons/registry.json` – the allocation list, by hand and **append only**: namespace (the mod id), short
  id for PW3 share codes (1 is Propwash itself, 2 Just More Parts; a short id is never given again), name, kinds
  (`parts`, `modes`, `accessories`, `tools`), page, license, lowest Propwash version, the product in `platforms.json`,
  the released versions, the hosted web export and the card text in English and German. An add-on that leaves the
  directory keeps its entry with `"status": "withdrawn"`.
- `assets/data/addons/<namespace>/<version>/` – the hosted web export of a version (format 2 of the mod's
  `/propwash webexport`: `manifest.json`, `parts.json`, `chemistries.json`, `mounts.json`, `share_table.json`), made
  from the published jar by the configurator's own jar reader; the manifest keeps the jar's name, size and SHA-256.
- `assets/data/addons/directory.json` – generated (format 1 of the PW3 design): every add-on with its short id, names,
  Modrinth and CurseForge slugs (from `platforms.json`) and its versions with the parts-table checksum and the hosted
  data. Propwash's `tools/sharecode/sync_directory.py --site <this file>` makes the directory copy in the mod from it.

```sh
node tools/build-addons.mjs --jar justmoreparts=<jar>   # hosted export of the registry's version from the jar,
                                                         # then directory.json and the pages
node tools/build-addons.mjs                              # directory.json and the regions of the pages
node tools/build-addons.mjs --check                      # lists what is out of date, writes nothing
node tools/test-addons.mjs [--release]                   # registry, append only, pages, hosted exports, texts
node tools/test-directory.mjs [--propwash-repo <dir>]    # short ids, parts tables only appended, Propwash's copy,
                                                         # sync_directory.py, /addons/ in headless Chrome
node tools/test-jar-loader.mjs --jmp-jar <jar> --propwash-jar <jar> --export <web export> [--propwash-repo <dir>]
```

The pages carry generated regions between `<!-- addons:<kind> [<namespace>] -->` and `<!-- /addons -->`: `cards
official|community`, `short-ids`, `facts`, `new` (parts whose table entry came with the listed version) and `parts`;
the cards hold the platform buttons of `tools/build-platforms.mjs`. For a release of an add-on: add the version to
`versions`, point `web_export` at it, run `build-addons.mjs --jar <ns>=<release jar>` and check with
`test-addons.mjs --release` (it refuses a hosted export built from a pre-release jar). A new add-on gets a new entry at
the end of `registry.json`, a page `addons/<name>/index.html` (in `HAND_PAGES` of `tools/build-nav.mjs` and the
sitemap) and its product in `platforms.json`.

**Add-on jars in the configurator.** The add-on bar above the part pickers shows the official add-ons with a switch
(off = as if the mod were not installed: its parts leave the pickers, codes with them read as from a missing add-on)
and “Open add-on jar” (file dialog or drag and drop). The jar is read only in the tab, nothing is uploaded or stored,
a reload forgets it, and no code from it runs: only `fabric.mod.json` or `META-INF/neoforge.mods.toml`, the part and
chemistry files, the parts table, the language files and the models, render definitions and textures the parts use.
Modules in `assets/js/configurator/`: `jar.js` (ZIP reader with limits: jar 64 MB, 20000 entries, 16 MB per entry,
JSON 2 MB, textures 4 MB and 1024 px, 128 MB unpacked, ratio 100:1 per entry; paths with `..`, absolute paths and
backslashes are skipped), `partjson.js` (port of `parts/PartJson` with the checks of the part records: every default,
`led_path`, the same refusals), `catalog-build.js` (what the web export makes of the files – `parts.json`,
`chemistries.json`, `mounts.json` – and the catalog entries and model files of the build tools), `addon-catalog.js`
(a jar as a piece of the catalog, with messages instead of errors), `directory.js` (registry and directory, the checks
shared with the tests) and `composeCatalog`/`composeRender` in `data.js`; the bar itself is
`assets/js/addons/configurator-addons.js`, the page script of `/addons/` `assets/js/addons/directory-page.js`, the
styles `assets/css/addons.css`. Parts an add-on registers only in Java have no file: they show up as unknown, with the
note that the add-on needs a web export. `test-jar-loader.mjs` holds all of it against the game: the jars of Just More
Parts and Propwash give the export's `parts.json`, `chemistries.json`, `mounts.json` and parts table field for field
and the site's catalog entries and model files byte for byte, and made-up jars (zip bombs, a too large texture, 20001
entries, a broken ZIP, paths with `..`, HTML in names, …) end in a message.

## Roadmap

`roadmap/` shows what is out, what is being built and what comes later – with rough horizons, never dates. The page
has no content of its own: `assets/js/roadmap/view.js` draws everything from `assets/data/roadmap.json`, so an update
only edits that file (no HTML).

```jsonc
{
  "schema": 1,
  "horizons": [                      // fixed order: released, now, next, later, ideas (a horizon may be left out)
    {
      "id": "now", "icon": "prop",
      "label": { "en": "Now", "de": "Jetzt" },                         // small coloured line above the heading
      "title": { "en": "In the next release", "de": "Im nächsten Update" },
      "intro": { "en": "…", "de": "…" },                               // optional
      "link":  { "href": "https://…", "label": { "en": "…", "de": "…" } }, // optional button under the intro
      "groups": [
        {
          "version": "0.5.0",                                           // optional; a release
          "title": { "en": "Learn, race, explore", "de": "…" },         // optional
          "items": [
            { "id": "world-rules", "icon": "globe", "status": "progress",
              "title": { "en": "World rules", "de": "Welt-Regeln" },
              "text":  { "en": "One line for players.", "de": "Eine Zeile für Spieler." },
              "link":  { "href": "…", "label": { "en": "…", "de": "…" } } }   // optional
          ]
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

- **Status:** `done`, `progress` (in progress), `planned` or `idea`. Items under `released` are all `done`, items under
  `ideas` are all `idea`, and `idea` appears nowhere else.
- **Progress:** a group with two or more items gets a ring with one segment per item (done green, in progress amber,
  planned blue) and “done of total” in the middle. The panel at the top of the page shows the first group with a
  `version` under `now`; the status counts and the jump links are counted from the file as well.
- **Releasing:** set the finished items of the release to `done`, move its group from `now` to the top of `released`
  (newest first – older releases then fold into one line), give it the link `../changelog/#propwash-<version>` (label
  “Full changelog” / “Alle Änderungen”), move the next group up from `next` to `now`, and run the check below. Items
  that would give away a release (new features nobody has announced) only appear once it is out.
- **Download buttons:** `"download": "propwash"` on a horizon (the released one) draws the platform buttons of that
  product from `assets/data/platforms.json` (see [Platforms](#platforms)); a horizon may also carry one `link`.
- **Languages:** `view.js` draws every text through `assets/js/i18n.js` (`spans()`, `text()`, `href()`), so the
  generated language pages show the roadmap in their language; texts of the script are `{ en: '…', de: '…' }`
  literals with `{placeholders}`.
- **Icons:** the names are the `ri-*` symbols in the sprite at the top of `roadmap/index.html` (16 × 16, drawn with
  strokes like the configurator's icons). A new icon is a new `<symbol id="ri-…">` there.
- **Texts:** short and player-facing (title up to 48, text up to 130 characters), both languages, no HTML, no dates
  (years, quarters and month names are rejected).

```sh
node tools/test-roadmap.mjs   # validates roadmap.json, the icons, and that every page links to the roadmap
```

## Changelog

`changelog/` lists every release of Propwash FPV and its official add-ons point by point, newest first: Propwash: Just
More Parts, Propwash: Camera Drones and Propwash: Mob Defense (`PRODUCTS` in `tools/build-changelog.mjs`, ids `jmp`,
`camdrones` and `mobdefense`). Each release shows its title line, highlights, then New, Improved, Fixed, For server owners,
Compatibility and Known issues. A filter shows all products or one of them (*All* on its own row above the four
products), the version list links every release, and each release has a fixed anchor for roadmap cards and Discord
posts: `changelog/#propwash-0.6.0`, `changelog/#jmp-1.2.0`, `changelog/#camdrones-1.0.0`,
`changelog/#mobdefense-1.0.0`, and `changelog/#propwash`, `#jmp`, `#camdrones` or `#mobdefense` for a whole product. Each
product has its own colour and symbol (`productIcon` in `assets/js/changelog/view.js`, `--cl-<id>` in
`assets/css/changelog.css`).

Nothing on the page is written by hand. `tools/build-changelog.mjs` reads the player-facing `CHANGELOG.md` of the four
mods from git – by default at the newest release tag `v<x.y.z>`, so notes of an untagged release never show – and writes
`assets/data/changelog.json`:

```sh
# after tagging a release of Propwash or one of its add-ons
node tools/build-changelog.mjs                   # writes assets/data/changelog.json, lists entries without German text
node tools/build-changelog.mjs --add-missing     # adds those entries to tools/changelog/de.json with an empty value
node tools/test-changelog.mjs
node tools/test-cov-changelog.mjs                # releases of 0.6.0, Just More Parts 1.2.0 and the two new add-ons
```

The repositories default to `PROPWASH_SRC`, `JMP_SRC`, `CAMDRONES_SRC` and `MOBDEFENSE_SRC`, otherwise `propwash`,
`propwash-justmoreparts`, `propwash-camera` and `propwash-mobdefense` next to this repository (or next to its main
checkout when you work in a worktree); `--propwash <dir>`, `--jmp <dir>`, `--camdrones <dir>`, `--mobdefense <dir>` and
`--propwash-ref`, `--jmp-ref`, `--camdrones-ref`, `--mobdefense-ref <ref>` override them, `--check` only reports whether
the JSON is up to date. The page of 0.6.0 was built before the tags from the release commits:

```sh
node tools/build-changelog.mjs --propwash-ref bba73caea --jmp-ref 70da15d --camdrones-ref b807ab2 --mobdefense-ref 6b9b90e
```

A coming release is shown before its tag by reading `CHANGELOG.md` from the commit of the release candidate
(`--propwash-ref <commit>`; the JSON records the full hash). `test-changelog.mjs` accepts that only while the version
shown is not tagged: once `v<x.y.z>` exists it fails until the page is rebuilt from the tag (plain
`node tools/build-changelog.mjs`). Links to repository files in a changelog (`[ADDONS.md](ADDONS.md)`) lead to this site
where the file has a place (`REPO_LINKS` in `tools/build-changelog.mjs`: `ADDONS.md` → `docs/api/#rules`) and otherwise
to the file in the public source repository.

`CHANGELOG.md` format: `## [x.y.z] — Title` per release (`–` or `-` before the title work as well, as in the
changelogs of 0.6.0 and the add-ons; `## [Unreleased]` and other non-version headings are skipped),
`### Highlights|New|Improved|Fixed|For server owners|Compatibility|Known issues` and `- ` bullets with `**bold**`,
`*emphasis*`, `` `code` `` and `[links](https://…)`; as in Markdown, a `*` with spaces on both sides is plain text
(“keypad / * −”). Anything else inside a release stops the build and names the line. Highlights start with their name in
bold (`**Flight training:** …`); the page shows that name as the card title. A repository without a release tag yet
(Camera Drones and Mob Defense before their first tag `v1.0.0`) needs its `--<id>-ref`, otherwise the build stops and
says so.

German: the changelogs are English, the German texts live in `tools/changelog/de.json` – per product and version, the
English source line as key and its German text as value. A line repeated word for word in a later release reuses its
translation. `test-changelog.mjs` fails while an entry has no German text, while a German text is no longer used (after a
line changed), when the JSON differs from a fresh build or a newer release tag exists, and checks the navigation and footer
links of every page and that the released groups of the roadmap link to `../changelog/#propwash-<version>`.

## Cache busting

GitHub Pages serves this site with `max-age=600` caching. Without cache busting, a browser that already cached
`assets/css/style.css` or a configurator script keeps using that cached file for up to 10 minutes after a new HTML
page of the same release has already loaded – new tiles get squeezed, the footer wraps one word per line, until a
hard reload. `tools/stamp-assets.mjs` fixes this by giving every local reference to `assets/css/*.css` and
`assets/js/**/*.js` a version query derived from the content it currently points to, e.g. `style.css?v=a1b2c3d4` (8
hex characters of a sha-256 of the file): a page always pulls the exact CSS/JS that was live when it was generated,
whatever a browser still has cached under the old, unversioned or differently stamped URL.

```sh
node tools/stamp-assets.mjs          # restamp every page and script that is out of date
node tools/stamp-assets.mjs --check  # report what is out of date and exit 1, writes nothing (used by the test below)
node tools/test-stamp-assets.mjs     # every local css/js reference, on every page and between the scripts, carries the current hash
```

Stamped: every `<link rel="stylesheet">`, `<link rel="modulepreload">` and `<script src>`/`<script type="module"
src>` of a local `assets/css/*.css` or `assets/js/**/*.js` file, on every page including the ones
`tools/build-docs.mjs` generates – and the ES module imports *between* the configurator's own scripts (`import …
from './data.js'`, the dynamic `import('./viewer.js')` the 3D view uses, and the changelog's and roadmap's `view.js`
→ `data.js`). That last part matters because the configurator and the roadmap/changelog pages are themselves ES
modules that import several sibling scripts directly – a stale cached `data.js` loaded alongside a fresh `ui.js`
would be exactly the same kind of broken-layout bug, just inside the configurator instead of on the page. A script's
hash also changes whenever a module it imports changes (computed in dependency order, so the one import cycle in the
configurator – `sharecode.js` and `sharecode2.js` import each other – gets one shared hash for both instead of an
unsolvable loop), so editing `rules.js` alone still busts every page that eventually loads it through `ui.js`.
Imports of `assets/vendor/*` (three.js, pako) are left alone: out of scope, pinned and only touched by the rebuild
command further up.

`tools/build-docs.mjs` calls the same stamping step right after writing `docs/**/index.html`, so regenerated docs
pages keep current stamps without an extra step. After hand-editing any other HTML page or any CSS/JS file, rerun
`node tools/stamp-assets.mjs` before committing; `tools/test-stamp-assets.mjs` (part of the test suite) fails with
the list of files to restamp until you do. `tools/test-links.mjs` and the other tests already ignore the `?v=` query
when resolving a local reference.

## Languages

English and German are the official languages: every page carries both side by side and the EN/DE switch. Spanish,
French, Portuguese (Brazil), Polish and Russian are **generated trees** `/es/`, `/fr/`, `/pt-br/`, `/pl/`, `/ru/`
with one language per page, translated with AI. Every page of them has a banner under the header that says so and
that only English and German are official, with links to the same page in English and German. The add-on developer
docs (add-on API, API reference, parts format) and the legal pages stay English/German; links to them lead to the
root version.

```sh
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs                    # writes /<lang>/ and assets/i18n/<lang>.json for every active language
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs --check            # reports generated files that are out of date, writes nothing
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs --missing fr       # missing and stale texts of a language as JSON: the work list
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs --stats            # texts and words to translate, per source
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs --propwash <repo>  # tools/i18n/ui-terms.json from the mod's English language file
node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs --out <dir> --lang fr,ru --pseudo   # preview of any language in another folder
node tools/i18n/test-i18n.mjs                     # dictionaries, generated pages, fonts, denylist
node tools/test-layout.mjs                        # every page × language × 360/768/1100/1280 px in headless Chrome
```

- **Languages:** `tools/i18n/languages.json` – code, `hreflang`, `og:locale`, name, `active` and the drafts of the
  banner, the hint and the menu texts. A language goes live (`"active": true`) only when its dictionary covers every
  text; the build stops otherwise. Then run `node tools/build-nav.mjs` (language menu and `hreflang` of the
  hand-written pages), `node tools/build-docs.mjs --render-only`, `node tools/i18n/build-i18n.mjs` and
  `node tools/test-links.mjs --write-sitemap`.
- **What is translated:** `tools/i18n/pages.mjs` – the landing page, configurator, roadmap, changelog, the docs for
  players (overview, player guide, world rules, blackbox tuning), the brand kit and, once they exist, converter and
  add-on pages; and the texts the scripts draw: roadmap, changelog, platforms and add-on data (every `{en, de}` pair),
  the part texts of the configurator catalog (not the part names), `{ en: '…', de: '…' }` literals in the roadmap and
  changelog scripts, the `['English', 'Deutsch']` texts of `assets/js/configurator/i18n.js` and the texts of `main.js`.
- **Dictionaries:** `tools/i18n/dict/<lang>.json` – `{ "schema": 1, "lang": "fr", "source": "en", "entries": { … } }`,
  the English text (whitespace collapsed, markup kept) as key, like `tools/changelog/de.json`; an optional `glossary`
  holds fixed translations of that language. A changed English sentence is a new key: `--missing` lists it, and the old
  one as stale.
- **Terms that stay English:** `tools/i18n/glossary.json` (names, FPV words, share-code versions) and the names of the
  game's tabs, buttons, keys and world rules in `tools/i18n/ui-terms.json` (from the mod: the game is only English and
  German, players have to find the buttons). Part names stay English as well.
- **A generated page:** navigation and footer links in the language (`tools/site-nav.mjs`), every `lang="en"`/`lang="de"`
  pair becomes one element with the translation, translated `alt`/`aria-label`/`title`, title and meta texts, head
  with `pw-lang`, `pw-root`, canonical, `og:locale` and every `hreflang`, the banner, links (translated pages stay in
  the tree, everything else leads to the root), cache-busting stamps. Generated pages are never edited by hand.
- **Scripts:** `assets/js/i18n.js` gives the scripts that draw content the site root (`root('assets/data/…')`), the
  page language and the texts of a translated page (`load()`, `text()`, `spans()`, `href()`, `number()`): on the
  English/German pages both languages as before, on the others one language from `assets/i18n/<lang>.json`. The
  configurator and the converter go through `assets/js/configurator/i18n.js`: on a translated page `t()` gives the
  translation of the English text, `tr()` that of a text from the web export (help, rules, warnings, statuses, tune
  groups, paint slots; part and preset names stay English), `num()` writes numbers in the language and `pageHref()` keeps links to
  translated pages in the language tree; both pages call `loadTexts()` before their first text.
- **Head block:** `node tools/build-nav.mjs` also writes the head of the hand-written pages (`siteHead` in
  `tools/site-nav.mjs`, found by its marker `<script data-pw-head>`): `pw-root`, and once another language is active
  the `hreflang` lines and `assets/css/i18n.css`; the head script reads `?lang=en|de` (the links from the other
  languages), keeps the choice and removes it from the address.
- **Language menu and hint:** the globe in the navigation lists English, Deutsch and the active languages (with the
  short “AI” in each language); a choice is kept in `propwash-lang` like EN/DE. There is no automatic redirect: on an
  English/German page a browser in another language without a stored choice sees one line that the page exists in
  that language; closing it keeps English or German. When the navigation does not fit in the header bar (longer words),
  `main.js` folds it into the menu button.
- **Fonts:** `saira-var.woff2` and `jetbrains-mono-var.woff2` cover Latin, Latin Extended-A (Polish) and the key
  symbols; Saira has no Cyrillic, so the Russian pages take the Cyrillic letters from Exo 2
  (`exo2-cyrillic-var.woff2`) and JetBrains Mono (`jetbrains-mono-cyrillic-var.woff2`), declared in
  `assets/css/i18n.css` for `html[lang="ru"]` only. Cut from the OFL sources (google/fonts, `ofl/saira`,
  `ofl/jetbrainsmono`, `ofl/exo2`) with `python3 tools/i18n/subset-fonts.py --src <folder>` (needs `fonttools` and
  `brotli`); `test-i18n.mjs` reads the fonts' character maps and fails when a letter of a language is missing.
- **Tests:** `test-i18n.mjs` checks the module, the sources, a pseudo build of all five languages and, per active
  language, 100 % coverage, no stale entries, the same tags, links, numbers, units and code spans as the English text,
  glossary and game terms unchanged, no English left, banner and `hreflang` of every page, and the local denylist (the
  one of the mod's public export, plus `~/.config/propwash/i18n-denylist.txt` for names of models and providers, if
  present; neither is in this repository). `test-layout.mjs` builds all five languages with pseudo translations where
  a text has none (their letters, 20 to 30 % longer), opens every page in every language at 360, 768, 1100 and 1280
  px and checks overflow, cut-off buttons, the header bar, the banner, the fonts actually drawn and the language
  menu; it keeps screenshots for the reviewers in a temporary folder.

`https://propwashfpv.com/changelog/`.

## Link previews

`og:image` and `twitter:image` need an absolute URL. They point to
`https://propwashfpv.com/assets/img/og-image.jpg`. With a different repository name (or a custom domain),
change the URLs in the `<head>` of `index.html`, `configurator/index.html`, `roadmap/index.html` and `changelog/index.html`,
`SITE` in `tools/docs/html.mjs` and `tools/test-links.mjs`, and `sitemap.xml` / `robots.txt`. Every page also carries its
canonical address and `og:url`.
